lens: pass-through layers.

a layer earns its place by *changing* something- representation, invariant, failure
mode, trust level. a layer that receives X, renames it, and hands it to the next
layer is typing practice.

method- pick a user-visible operation (submit tx, run inference, fetch result) and
trace it top to bottom, writing down every function boundary it crosses. for each
boundary ask: what does the callee know or enforce that the caller could not? if the
answer is nothing, the boundary is a candidate for collapse. adaptors that adapt an
adaptor are the strongest signal. count the wrapper structs whose only field is the
thing they wrap and whose methods all delegate.

do not collapse a boundary that exists for a *trust* reason (kernel vs host, signed
vs unsigned data) even if it looks like a pass-through today.

output: the call-trace with each boundary marked keep/collapse and why, then the
collapse as separate commits, deepest layer first.
